Italy's largest bank more than doubles crypto holdings in Q1 (4 minute read) Intesa Sanpaolo, Italy's largest bank, more than doubled its crypto holdings from approximately $100 million at the end of 2025 to around $235 million as of March 31, driven by expanded Bitcoin ETF positions, first-time entries into Ethereum through BlackRock's iShares Staked Ethereum Trust and XRP via the Grayscale XRP Trust ETF (approximately $26 million), and its first derivatives play with iShares Bitcoin Trust call options. The bank simultaneously executed a near-total exit from Solana, slashing its Bitwise Solana Staking ETF position from 266,320 shares to just 2,817, while making strategic adjustments to crypto equity holdings including adding 165,600 BitGo shares, dumping its Bitmine position, closing Strategy put options, and increasing Coinbase shares from 1,500 to 10,357. Intesa's positions are held for proprietary trading purposes and the expansion follows Ripple's announcement last month that it would offer custody services to the Italian banking group, signaling deepening institutional engagement with digital assets.

Introducing Agentic Wallet Skills (4 minute read) molty.cash has open-sourced agentic-wallets-skills, a reusable SKILL catalog documenting wallet detection, balance probing, and payment flows for 10 agent-native wallets, including bankr, circle, lobstercash, and agentcash, across 7 settlement chains with x402 and MPP support. The project targets a recurring failure mode in paid HTTP endpoints: when agents encounter a 402 response, they cannot determine which installed wallet CLI supports the required chain or protocol, causing retry loops and endpoint abandonment. Each SKILL file specifies detection logic, balance probing, and the exact call sequence for paid endpoints, with the catalog designed for any operator rather than tied to molty.cash infrastructure. The release is an early community effort to standardize wallet discovery and transport compatibility for the machine-payable web.

Proof: A New Type of Exchange (3 minute read) Proof is launching Multiverse Markets, a conditional perpetual that prices what an asset is worth in the world where a specific event occurs, not the probability of the event itself. Each contract settles to the underlying perpetual if the event occurs and returns capital if it does not, enabling leverage and cross-margining that binary $0-or-$1 structures on Polymarket and Kalshi cannot support. Target markets include SPY conditional on election outcomes, TSMC conditional on a Taiwan blockade, and BTC conditional on quantum computing advances, addressing the gap where economically significant events have lagged behind short-term speculative markets in prediction market volume.

APYX combined TVL grew from $26M at the March 4 Pendle listing to $371M by May 12, a 14.3x increase in under 2.5 months, with Pendle holding approximately $296M, or 76.7% of APYX's total TVL. Pearl Launches L1 With Proof-of-Useful-Work (5 minute read) Pearl (PRL) is a Bitcoin L1 fork that replaces SHA-256 with matrix multiplication as its proof-of-work mechanism, enabling GPU cycles running AI inference and training workloads to mine PRL while simultaneously securing the network. The OTC price moved from $0.30 to $0.70 in four days, while aggregate network production cost climbed from $0.30 to $0.51 in 72 hours, a 70% increase that the whitepaper attributes to the core property of proof-of-useful-work: in equilibrium, the price floor tracks the marginal cost of the underlying compute. PRL has no CEX listing, no DEX pool, no Coingecko or CoinMarketCap entry, with all current price discovery occurring OTC. The design positions Pearl as a monetary asset whose cost of production is the marginal cost of GPU compute, the input resource that underwrites AI inference at scale.

The Wallet UX Problem May Be Unfixable (2 minute read) Wallet UX confusion runs deeper than most crypto insiders recognize, as people without a technical background find even basic wallet interactions genuinely incomprehensible. Embedded wallets paper over the problem but introduce their own failure model as they are effectively custodial, and users hit a wall when they need to extract funds from a specific app. The result is a structural trap for crypto app developers to either preserve self-custody and dramatically cap your addressable market, or adopt embedded wallets and blur the line between crypto infrastructure and custodial fintech.

a16z Crypto: The CLARITY Act Is Crypto's 1933 Moment (3 minute read) The Senate Banking Committee advanced the Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act on May 14 in a bipartisan vote, building on the House's earlier passage (294-134) and years of legislative groundwork stretching back to Lummis-Gillibrand in 2022. a16z General Counsel Miles Jennings frames CLARITY as a generational regulatory reset, comparable to the Securities Act of 1933, that would end a decade of regulation-by-enforcement, draw a clear SEC/CFTC jurisdictional line, and give blockchain networks a legal pathway to operate domestically without structural compromise. Existing law was built for companies, not networks: forcing blockchain projects into corporate form concentrates control, creates intermediaries, and defeats the decentralization that makes crypto valuable in the first place. If CLARITY clears a full Senate vote and the House, builders gain the same kind of clarity the GENIUS Act gave stablecoin issuers which a16z credits with unlocking an overnight wave of mainstream adoption.
