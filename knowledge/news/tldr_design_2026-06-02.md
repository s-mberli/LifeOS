Autodesk Buys MaintainX for $3.6bn to Push from Design Into Operations (2 minute read) Autodesk is acquiring MaintainX, a maintenance and operations platform, for $3.6 billion in an all-cash deal. Founded in 2018 and last privately valued at $2.5 billion, MaintainX serves over 500,000 frontline workers with mobile-first maintenance software. The purchase fills the missing "operate" layer in Autodesk's design-to-operations strategy, while also giving it a stream of maintenance data to fuel its AI ambitions.

The Part of Agile Designers Fear the Most: Imperfect Work (4 minute read) Agile's promise of continuous improvement exposes designers' deepest fear — shipping imperfect work — because teams frequently move on without iterating on released features. The word "viable" in MVP is often sidelined by "minimum," yet a poorly designed release yields unreliable feedback — users reject the experience, not the idea. True iteration — cycles of release, observation, and structural refinement — is what makes early releases worth shipping, freeing designers to build responsibly for the present.

Designing how designers master AI (13 minute read) A Salesforce UX team discovered that mastering AI tools is less about learning fixed workflows and more about developing personal ways of working that amplify individual strengths, experiences, and thinking styles. Through six months of peer-led learning sessions, they found that the most valuable knowledge came not from formal training but from colleagues sharing real-world experiments, failures, and workflows, helping others see "a window into someone's thinking" and build their own relationship with AI tools.

Designing for AI Means Designing Like it's 1999 (13 minute read) The unsettled state of AI design mirrors the early web of 1999 — standards still shifting, no killer app yet, business models unproven. Chat is today's default interface but likely a placeholder, since 95% of generative-AI pilots still deliver no measurable business impact. Designers who thrive will treat this moment as an invitation to invent new conventions, just as their predecessors did in that earlier era.
