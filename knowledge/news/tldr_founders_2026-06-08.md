### The startup that launched itself
- **URL:** https://www.opensourceceo.com/p/andrew-busse-interview?utm_source=tldrfounders
- **Via:** TLDR Founders, 2026-06-08
- **Read time:** 8 minute read
- **TLDR Summary:** A startup inside Airtable ran its own launch almost entirely on its own agents, from cold outreach to the landing page to qualifying leads. ### AI just took 40 of the App Store's top 100
- **URL:** https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/app-store-ecosystem-reaches-1-point-4-trillion-usd-as-developers-thrive-globally/?utm_source=tldrfounders

### The Inevitable Failure of One-Shot Project Funding
- **URL:** https://www.mironov.com/inevitable/?utm_source=tldrfounders
- **Via:** TLDR Founders, 2026-06-08
- **Read time:** 6 minute read
- **TLDR Summary:** Software requires ongoing investment, but this is a feature, not a bug or mistake. Software is a depreciating intellectual asset, and companies need to recognize and budget for this. Every line of code in software needs to be supported until the last customer deletes it. One-time project-based budgets ignore this reality, which inevitably leads to financial and technical crises.

### Your AI strategy has a trust problem, not a tooling problem

### Your next SEO hire is a product manager
- **URL:** https://www.productledseo.com/p/who-should-you-hire-for-aeo?utm_source=tldrfounders
- **Via:** TLDR Founders, 2026-06-08
- **Read time:** 7 minute read
- **TLDR Summary:** Search is moving from ten blue links to AI answers, and the usual SEO hire, a channel manager who files tickets and reports rankings, is built for the old game. What works now is product thinking, understanding what a person actually means when they ask, then shaping the product so an AI model trusts it enough to cite. AI made the technical grunt work cheap, so hire for judgment, the person who can walk into a product review and defend a roadmap, not a keyword report.

### We Accidentally Built a Second Codebase
- **URL:** https://akshay.co/posts/accidental-second-codebase/?utm_source=tldrfounders
- **Via:** TLDR Founders, 2026-06-08
- **Read time:** 10 minute read
- **TLDR Summary:** Skills are small bundles of instructions handed to models so they can run the same workflows every time. Teams will often notice they keep doing the same things every time, so they write the workflow down as a skill and stop doing it by hand. Over time, this can result in a huge library of skills. These skills will eventually become obsolete as models improve. Developers need to monitor skill development so they don't build up and become a second code base that nobody uses.
