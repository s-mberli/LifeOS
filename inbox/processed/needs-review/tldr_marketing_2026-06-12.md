# Loewe's TikTok strategy 🤳, LinkedIn Creator Marketplace 🤝, when OOH works 🪧

## Summary
This resource is a curated marketing briefing covering several distinct but interconnected themes: the shifting landscape of social media strategy, the evolving role of AI in marketing and the workforce, and tactical conversion optimization techniques. The central worldview is that authenticity, speed, and strategic alignment outperform polish, scale, and imitation. Loewe's TikTok strategy is held up as a case study in how low-production, iPhone-shot content can generate 40 million organic views by breaking through the noise of over-produced brand content—but the critical enabler is not the tool or format, it's internal organizational trust that empowers teams to act on creative instincts without layers of approval. This principle of speed-over-perfection is a recurring theme. On AI, the resource highlights both opportunity and risk: Anthropic is proactively shaping the policy conversation around AI-driven job displacement by proposing wage insurance, retraining, and eventually AI taxes or UBI—a trust-building move that other companies may need to emulate. For product marketers, AI's highest value is in automating repetitive tasks (research, content repurposing) to free up time for strategic work like positioning and customer insight, not replacing judgment. Meanwhile, the talent pipeline is under threat as entry-level marketing roles vanish due to automation, risking long-term erosion of irreplaceable skills like judgment, creativity, and relationships. The solution proposed is to redesign how junior talent is deployed rather than waiting for market correction. On the tactical side, splitting checkout into two steps (email opt-in before payment) followed by automated abandoned cart emails can boost conversions by 10–20% as an evergreen system. OOH advertising (billboards) is framed as effective only when aligned with audience, business model, creative strengths, and budget—not when done because competitors are doing it. Bay Area costs of $20K–$50K/month demand clear goals and multi-channel coordination. LinkedIn's new Creator Marketplace is an invite-only platform connecting creators with brands for partnerships, with creators opting in via a monetization tab. Additional notes include Pinterest enabling Amazon Storefront links for creators, Ramp's innovative 3D direct mail card, and World Cup ad trends favoring fan experience over product or celebrity.

## Key Ideas
- Authenticity over polish in social media: Loewe's iPhone-shot TikTok video generated 40M organic views by standing out against over-produced brand content. The key enabler is internal trust—empowering teams to act quickly on creative instincts without excessive approval processes. Applicable to any brand willing to trade control for resonance.
- AI for product marketers: AI's highest-value application is automating repetitive tasks (research, content repurposing, competitive analysis) to free up time for strategic work like positioning, customer insight, and storytelling. AI should augment judgment, not replace it.
- Abandoned cart conversion fix: Splitting checkout into two steps—email opt-in first, then payment details—followed by automated abandoned cart email sequences can increase conversions by 10–20%. This runs as an evergreen system requiring minimal ongoing maintenance once set up.
- OOH advertising strategic alignment: Billboards only work when there is clear alignment between the audience, business model, creative strengths, and budget. At $20K–$50K/month in the Bay Area, OOH demands clear goals and should be paired with coordinated multi-channel efforts (LinkedIn, events, product launches) rather than used in isolation.
- LinkedIn Creator Marketplace: An invite-only platform connecting creators with brands for partnerships including ads, speaking engagements, and collaborations. Creators opt in via a monetization tab, making their profiles discoverable to brands based on content quality and audience fit.
- Talent pipeline crisis in marketing: Entry-level marketing roles are disappearing due to automation, threatening the development of irreplaceable skills—judgment, creativity, and relationships—that are best learned on the job. The solution is to redesign junior talent deployment proactively rather than waiting for market correction.
- Anthropic's proactive AI policy positioning: By publishing a paper proposing a three-stage response to AI-driven job disruption (wage insurance/retraining, public benefits, and eventually AI taxes or UBI), Anthropic is shaping regulation and building trust preemptively—a model for how AI companies can get ahead of criticism.
