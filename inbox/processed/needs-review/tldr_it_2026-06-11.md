# $35B AI infra push ⚡️, Zscaler targets AI agents 🤖, Ivanti patches Sentry flaws 🛡️

## Summary
This TLDR IT newsletter from June 11, 2026 captures a pivotal moment in the convergence of AI infrastructure buildout, agentic AI security, and enterprise governance. The headline story is the $35 billion AI XPV Platform launched by Broadcom, Apollo, and Blackstone — a massive private capital deployment targeting over 20 gigawatts of compute capacity by 2028 for frontier AI labs. This signals that AI infrastructure is entering a utility-scale investment phase, comparable to the buildout of cloud data centers in the 2010s, but at dramatically larger scale and speed. The implication is that compute availability will continue to expand rapidly, lowering barriers for AI-native products and services while concentrating power among well-capitalized players.

Simultaneously, the security landscape is being reshaped by the rise of agentic AI. Zscaler's launch of a zero trust platform specifically for AI agents — with AI Broker for MCP/A2A protocol security, Endpoint AI Security, AI Access Graph, and expanded AI Protect features (code scanning, prompt extraction, red teaming) — represents a structural shift: security tooling is no longer just protecting human-to-system interactions but must now govern autonomous agent-to-agent and agent-to-tool communications. This is a new attack surface that traditional security stacks were not designed for. The Ivanti Sentry patches (max-severity OS command injection and authentication bypass) underscore how critical infrastructure remains vulnerable to foundational exploits, even as the industry races toward AI-native architectures.

The newsletter also highlights three emerging systemic challenges. First, browser-based phishing is outpacing traditional security tools as workflows migrate to browsers — a sign that endpoint-centric security models are becoming obsolete. Second, shadow AI is amplifying pre-existing governance gaps in SaaS, identity, and access control, meaning organizations that haven't solved basic governance will find AI exponentially harder to manage. Third, ontology misalignment — the gap between how LLMs internally represent knowledge and how enterprises formally define their data structures — is identified as a core source of truth errors in agentic systems. This is perhaps the most technically profound insight: even with perfect data access, agents will produce wrong outputs if the semantic mapping between model understanding and enterprise definitions is flawed.

On the business model side, Salesforce's acquisition of m3ter (usage-based billing) to integrate into Agentforce Revenue Management reflects the broader industry shift from seat-based SaaS pricing to consumption and outcome-based models driven by AI. Monte Carlo's containerized agents and hybrid deployment options, F5's AI-powered WAF and virtual patching, Pinecone's open-source vector DB monitoring stack, and SafeAgentDB's per-agent-branch isolated databases all point to an enterprise AI tooling ecosystem maturing rapidly around deployment flexibility, observability, and security isolation.

## Key Ideas
- AI Infrastructure at Utility Scale: The $35B Broadcom/Apollo/Blackstone AI XPV Platform targeting 20+ GW by 2028 signals that AI compute is becoming a utility-scale infrastructure play. For builders, this means compute costs will likely decrease and availability will increase, but the moats will shift to orchestration, fine-tuning, and application-layer differentiation rather than raw compute access.
- Agentic AI Requires a New Security Stack: Zscaler's zero trust platform for AI agents (securing MCP/A2A protocols, agent endpoint security, access governance) demonstrates that the security model must evolve from protecting human-system interactions to governing autonomous agent-to-agent communications. The key protocols to watch are MCP (Model Context Protocol) and A2A (Agent-to-Agent) — these are becoming the new perimeter.
- Ontology Misalignment as a Core AI Risk: The gap between LLMs' latent knowledge representations and enterprise structural definitions is a fundamental source of truth errors in agentic systems. This means that even with perfect tool access and data connectivity, agents will hallucinate or produce incorrect outputs if the semantic mapping layer is not explicitly designed. This is a data architecture and knowledge engineering problem, not just a prompt engineering problem.
- Shadow AI Exposes Pre-existing Governance Gaps: Unmanaged AI adoption doesn't create new governance problems — it amplifies existing weaknesses in SaaS management, identity, and access control. Organizations with poor foundational governance will find AI exponentially harder to control. The fix is to solve basic governance first, then layer AI-specific controls on top.
- Usage-Based Billing is Becoming Native to AI Products: Salesforce acquiring m3ter for Agentforce signals that consumption-based pricing is becoming the default for AI-driven products. Any AI product or service should architect for usage metering and billing from day one, as the market expectation is shifting away from flat-rate or seat-based pricing.
- Browser-Centric Workflows Demand Browser-Native Security: Browser-based phishing outpacing traditional tools means that security strategies must shift toward browser-level controls, CASB, and inline inspection rather than relying on endpoint agents and email gateways alone.
