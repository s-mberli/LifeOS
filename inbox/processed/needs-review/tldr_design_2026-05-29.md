iOS 27 leak reveals new Siri design, Camera app, more (4 minute read) Bloomberg's new leaked illustrations of iOS 27 reveal Apple's biggest Siri redesign yet, with the assistant moving into the Dynamic Island and gaining a new “Search or Ask” interface that combines voice commands, typed requests, web search, and chatbot-style conversations. The McDonald's charity logo finally got the redesign it desperately needed (4 minute read) Ronald McDonald House Charities has introduced a long-overdue rebrand that replaces its outdated logo featuring Ronald McDonald's hand with a warmer, more modern and versatile identity better suited to its global mission of supporting families with ill children. The redesign keeps the charity's emotional warmth and connection to McDonald's through color and hand-drawn elements, while improving clarity, donor appeal, and independence as the organization expands toward its goal of doubling the number of families it serves by 2030.

I'm Making a Spec for Design System Documentation (6 minute read) The author is creating a Design System Documentation Spec to standardize how design system knowledge is documented and stored. The spec aims to solve issues like vendor lock-in, data portability, and fragmented documentation by providing a structured format with two main sections: entities and document blocks. This standardized approach would create a true single source of truth, enable easier tool building, and support AI integration through structured data.

Why I Don't Use Icon Fonts in Figma (5 minute read) Icon fonts in Figma require a cumbersome 7-step process that involves leaving the application to browse icons on external websites and copying tiny renderings. Users must memorize exact icon names without visual previews, creating a poor experience compared to SVG components with fuzzy search and instance-swapping capabilities. Icon fonts also make comparing different glyphs tedious since icons can't be easily placed side by side for visual comparison.

Why UX Should Own Retention (4 minute read) UX teams should own customer retention since acquiring new customers costs five times more than keeping existing ones, yet most organizations focus on acquisition while neglecting retention strategy. Retention currently falls between departments with no clear ownership, creating an opportunity for UX to claim this strategic responsibility and tie their work directly to revenue metrics. By owning retention, UX can shift from being perceived as making screens look nice to being seen as strategically important for business outcomes like lifetime customer value and churn reduction.

Designing Where the Pixels Actually Live (6 minute read) A senior product designer at Slack describes how AI tooling helped her bridge the longstanding gap between static design mockups and real, interactive product experiences. The transition came with trade-offs — AI occasionally breaks existing work and demands smaller, more deliberate iteration steps — but the ability to design closer to the real experience feels like a meaningful shift.

The revolution will not be optimised: on frictionmaxxing and the creative virtues of inconvenience (10 minute read) Modern life is increasingly designed to eliminate friction and inconvenience, but in doing so it may also be removing the pauses, boredom, and small inefficiencies that foster creativity, intimacy, reflection, and human connection. The growing “frictionmaxxing” trend argues that some forms of slowness and inconvenience — like walking to a shop, cooking, waiting, or doing things manually — are not obstacles to life but essential parts of experiencing it fully, pushing back against a culture obsessed with constant optimisation and productivity.
