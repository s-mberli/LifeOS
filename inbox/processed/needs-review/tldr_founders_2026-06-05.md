# Google Search profiles 🔍, the AI treadmill 🏃, outcome pricing design 💸

## Summary
This TLDR Founders digest from June 5, 2026 captures a pivotal moment in the AI and SaaS landscape, where the industry is grappling with the paradox of AI-driven efficiency: speed and automation are table stakes, but the real competitive advantage lies in how founders use the cognitive space that AI frees up. The newsletter weaves together three macro-themes — platform shifts (Google Search Profiles), philosophical reframing (the AI Treadmill), and business model innovation (Outcome-Based Pricing) — into a coherent worldview: the next wave of winning companies won't be the ones that ship fastest, but the ones that think deepest, price most intelligently, and build for tomorrow's AI capabilities rather than today's.

Google's introduction of Search Profiles represents a significant platform shift that gives creators and website owners direct control over their Search presence via Knowledge Panels, Discover, and dedicated URLs. This is Google's answer to the creator economy's demand for identity and discoverability within its ecosystem — essentially turning Search into a semi-social platform. For founders, this creates a new SEO/visibility lever that didn't exist before.

The 'AI Treadmill' concept is the philosophical core of the piece. The argument is that AI's automation of repetitive work creates a fork in the road: either you fill the freed-up time with more tasks (the treadmill) or you use it for deep, creative, strategic thinking. The piece warns that shipping faster with AI can actually degrade product quality when speed replaces thought. This aligns with a broader pattern noted across the newsletter — that 'apps are becoming memes,' where software is so cheap to build that virality and taste matter more than roadmaps. The implication is that taste, judgment, and strategic clarity are becoming the scarcest resources.

Outcome-Based Pricing is presented as the next evolution of SaaS monetization, with Ben Murray's 12 design decisions framework providing a rigorous blueprint. The key distinction is between true outcome pricing (paying for verified results) versus consumption-based pricing dressed up as outcome pricing. Real-world examples span from Intercom Fin at $0.99 per outcome to EVE CoreGuard's $37.5K–$1.2M risk-avoidance ladder, showing the model's flexibility across price points and industries. Critical design elements include verification windows (e.g., Zendesk's 72-hour LLM-verified resolution), training periods (Help Scout's free 3-month window), and risk-tiered pricing.

The piece also covers important market dynamics: DeepSeek's rise as the top trending SaaS on Ramp signals a shift toward open-source AI models; Asana's Agentic Work Management Platform addresses enterprise AI failures through organizational memory and governance; B2B buying now requires unified narratives tailored across multiple stakeholders; and 'skin in the game' is no longer a sufficient signal of founder commitment due to easy monetization via secondaries and SPVs.
