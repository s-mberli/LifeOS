# Earning AI opinions 🤖, VC liquidity crisis 📉, rebuilding customer success 🤝

## Summary
This TLDR Founders newsletter from June 12, 2026 captures a pivotal moment in the startup and AI landscape where several macro forces are converging. The central thesis is that we are in a period of massive transition — in how companies build with AI, how capital flows (or doesn't), and how customer relationships are managed — and that founders who understand these shifts early will compound advantages while those who don't will accumulate hidden costs.

The VC liquidity crisis is the most structurally significant theme. Despite record paper valuations — over 1,500 unicorns representing roughly $6 trillion in notional value — actual cash returns to LPs have stalled dramatically because IPO and M&A markets remain largely closed. Secondary market data reveals the truth: unicorn stakes trade at 40–60% discounts to official marks. At current exit rates, clearing the backlog would take an estimated 49 years. This creates a dangerous disconnect between perception and reality, where fund announcements and paper wealth mask a system that is functionally illiquid. For founders, this means fundraising environments will remain tough, down rounds will be common, and the companies that survive will be those that can demonstrate real unit economics and a credible path to cash returns rather than relying on narrative-driven markups.

On the AI cost front, the newsletter identifies a widespread and expensive mistake: companies using frontier models (GPT-4 class) for every task, regardless of complexity. The fix is straightforward — smart model routing. Use open-source models (often 90% cheaper and nearly as capable) for routine work, and reserve premium models only for genuinely complex reasoning. Companies that implement this can cut AI spend by up to 93% overnight without meaningful performance loss. Equally important is the incentive design: rewarding raw token usage encourages waste, while rewarding impact per person drives efficiency. This is a direct operational lever that any AI-forward company can pull immediately.

The customer success section argues that the traditional CSM role is being disrupted by Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE), which has seen 1,000%+ growth in job postings. The data is damning for traditional CSM: NPS is widely discredited as a retention predictor, and over 50% of CSM activities show no correlation with retention. The Weave case study is the proof point — by shifting from a 'closed-won' mindset to measuring 'implementation success thresholds,' they dropped monthly churn from 4% to ~0.5% while scaling from $8M to $200M ARR. The lesson is that customers don't churn because they're unhappy — they churn because they never achieved meaningful value from the product.

The AI notetaker theme reframes how companies should think about institutional knowledge. With tools like Otter.ai, Fireflies, and others making meeting recording the default, companies that treat AI as a permanent participant — onboarding it into culture, decisions, and context — will build compounding knowledge advantages. Talk-heavy cultures like Shopify and OpenAI are already benefiting. The metaphor is powerful: treat AI notetakers like a new hire who attends every meeting and remembers everything.

Finally, the newsletter makes a nuanced point about earning AI opinions. In a landscape full of tribalism and hype, valuable AI perspectives come from hands-on use and evidence-based updating, not from identity or proximity to AI companies. Being early matters less than staying clear-headed. The real competitive moat is 'above the model' — the human creativity, judgment, and institutional knowledge that AI cannot replicate but can amplify.
